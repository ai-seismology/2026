About the workshop

Overview

This one-day workshop bridges industry and academia, exploring recent advances in AI-driven seismology and their practical applications — from seismic detection and source characterisation to subsurface imaging and safe underground energy storage.

Organised by the Department of Physical Sciences and Engineering at Royal Holloway, University of London, the workshop brings together speakers from Shell, UCL, the North Sea Transition Authority (NSTA) and the National Physical Laboratory (NPL) to foster dialogue between academia and industry.

10:15 Talk #1
Seismology in the Age of AI: Large-Scale Applications and Future Frontiers
Ana Ferreira, UCL
AI is rapidly transforming seismology, enabling large-scale earthquake detection and cataloguing, new insights into earthquake physics and tectonics, improved uncertainty quantification and advances in seismic imaging and inversion. This talk will review these successes and emerging scientific discoveries, before examining the challenges and limitations of current approaches, including generalisation, observational bias, uncertainty and the integration of physical knowledge.

I will then discuss future directions, including physics-informed AI, AI-accelerated simulations and inversions, seismic foundation models and applications to poorly observed environments such as the oceans and deep Earth. The talk will conclude by considering how AI might evolve from a powerful data analysis tool into a genuine engine for scientific discovery in seismology.

11:45 Talk #3
Improving automated characterisation of seismic signals with AI: from moment tensors to whale calls
Alex Saoulis, UCL
Seismograms contain a wealth of information, but analysis is complicated by overlapping sources of noise and uncertainties about the inner Earth structure. We show how AI can enable more accurate and more automated characterisation of range of seismic signals. We demonstrate that simulation-based inference (SBI), an approach to Bayesian inference that uses machine learning models to better quantify uncertainty, can produce substantially more accurate moment tensor solutions than traditional approaches at a fraction of the computational cost. In addition, we also show how modern AI techniques such as semantic segmentation can enable automated characterisation of more exotic seismic signals, such as ocean currents and whale calls.
12:15 Talk #4
Monitoring of Induced Seismicity – A journey from expert-dependent to autonomous methods
Chris Willacy, Shell
This presentation will discuss the requirements for induced seismicity monitoring in industrial applications and how the technology is evolving from expert-dependent workflows toward AI-enabled autonomous systems. Using experience from monitoring the giant Groningen gas field with elastic full waveform inversion, the talk will illustrate the complexity of field-scale operations and the challenges that must be addressed to enable the next generation of microseismic monitoring applications.

14:00 Talk #5
Simulation-Based Inference for De-risking CCS and Seismic Source Inversion: Groningen Case Study
Carolina M. García López, Royal Holloway
Monitoring induced seismicity is essential for de-risking Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) operations. This presentation will introduce simulation-based inference (SBI), a machine-learning approach to seismic source inversion, through the case study of the Groningen gas field. It will explore how SBI can make this necessary task faster and more robust to non-Gaussian sources of error, while amortising the upfront training cost across many events instead of solving each one from scratch.
14:30 Talk #6
Science with seafloor cables
Giuseppe Marra, NPL
The ability to transform ocean-crossing seafloor fibre-optic cables into environmental sensor arrays offers a unique opportunity to close the deep-ocean observational gap. By providing continuous measurements from the ocean floor, these systems could transform seismology, oceanography, and climate research. However, fully realizing this potential will require extracting meaningful insights from vast amount of seafloor data and a very diverse range of signals. AI and machine learning will be indispensable tools for identifying, characterizing, and interpreting signals at unprecedented speed and scale, accelerating science advances and likely new discoveries.
